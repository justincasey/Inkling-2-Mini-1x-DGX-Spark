# Numerics of the reference forward (oracle)

This file fixes the numerics of `oracle/` (the CPU reference that the REXL3 `inkling2_loop` family is checked against) for the Mini. It answers
`docs/PORT-SPEC.md` section 9 (UNKNOWN-1 to UNKNOWN-21), lists the oracle's rounding points and the lossy choices the engine makes, and follows the Small's
`docs/NUMERICS.md` (key SMALL) wherever the Mini is identical. Citation keys are those of PORT-SPEC section 0 (HFL, HFS, HFX, VLM, PR181, PR249, PACK, SMALL, ENG, A.n =
the appendix of PORT-SPEC); a locator is `file:lines` at the pinned revision, or `n/` for `vllm/models/inkling2/nvidia/` and `cuda/` for `crates/cuda/` in ENG.

**Principle** (unchanged from the Small). The oracle evaluates every op of PORT-SPEC section 3 in float64 starting from the stored values and rounds only at the spec's
storage points: to **bf16** (through fp32, `bf16(fp32(x))`) wherever HFL stores bf16, and to **fp32** wherever HFL names an fp32 value that crosses an op boundary (the AttnRes
mixes, the loop messages and blends, the partial `P + a`, the router, the expert sum, the logits). Transcendentals are precise: no approximate instructions, no flush to zero.
Products of bf16 or fp16 values are exact in float64, so a result does not depend on summation order, batch size or chunking, and prefill plus decode reproduces a full
recompute bit for bit. Byte-exact serving is a property of the engine against its own pinned build; the oracle is the tolerance anchor.

## Other fixed choices

- **Visit maps and AttnRes state** (PORT-SPEC 3.2, 3.3): the oracle executes the 48 visits in order with `B` and `P` exactly as in the pseudo-code; each pass restarts from the prelude's `(B, P)`; messages of pass `p` come from pass `p - 1`; the coda continues from pass 3. `P` and every `B` entry are bf16; `partial` (the MLP-mix input) is the fp32 `fp32(P) + fp32(a)` on non-boundary layers and `fp32(a)` on boundary layers. At most 5 sources per mix (A.13).
- **AttnRes mix** (3.3): normalized sources `x_n * rsqrt(mean(x_n^2) + 1e-6)` are both keys and values; the output is fp32 (rounded from float64) and is consumed unrounded by the next op: a controller blend, or the RMSNorm with gamma whose output is stored bf16 (`bf16( rsqrt-normalized(d) * fp32(w) )`).
- **Loop messages** (3.4): the message mix of a loop layer yields four fp32 outputs (q, k, v, mlp) from the 2-source bank of the previous pass (queries merged as in HFX `core_model_loading:823-853`, then pre-scaled, UNKNOWN-10); the controller pair-norm input is fp32; `n = bf16([rms(dest), rms(msg)])`; `hdn = bf16( n . down^T )`; `gain` and `forget` are `fp32( hdn . W^T )` from the bf16 weights (HF's fp32 copies are the same values); `blend = (1 - tanh(forget)) * dest + tanh(gain) * msg` is evaluated in float64 and stored fp32 (VLM computes it in fp32 without FMA: a difference of fp32 rounding only); the blend feeds the layer's RMSNorm, whose output is bf16. For a sliding loop layer the one mix is blended three times; the three normalized outputs are the q, k and v stream inputs.
- **Router** (3.10): logits `fp32( fp32(y) . fp32(W_gate)^T )`; selection by `sigmoid(logit) + bias` in float64 with a stable top-8; weights `fp32( 8 * sigmoid_j / ( sum_sel sigmoid + 1e-20 ) )`.
- **Experts**: `softcap(g, 4) * sigmoid(g) * softcap(u, 25)` in float64 from fp32 `g`, `u`, rounded to bf16; `K` per tensor is read from the trellis and only selects the decoded weights.
- **Log scaling** (3.7): `tau = fp32( 1 + 0.1 * ln( max( (pos + 1) / 8192, 1 ) ) )`, applied after the clip and its bf16 rounding: `bf16(q̂ * tau)`, `bf16(r̂ * tau)`; exactly 1 up to position 8191 (A.14); global layers only.
- **l2 clip** `x * min(c / (||x||_2 + 1e-6), 1)` and the value norm `x * rsqrt(mean(x^2) + 1e-6)`: float64, stored bf16.
- **No TP emulation**: the Small's `tp = 4` rank partials are gone; the `wo` and MoE outputs are rounded once to bf16. The oracle has no `--tp` option for the Mini.
- **Vocabulary**: the unembed is evaluated for the first 200,058 output rows only (the engine computes 12,504 sixteen-wide output tiles = 200,064 columns, PORT-SPEC 3.12); logits beyond 200,057 do not exist.
- **Dense BF16 tensors from the pack**: the routers, `attn_sink_proj`, `weight_proj`, controllers and convs enter as their stored bf16 values (upcast exact); AttnRes queries enter as fp32.
- **Vision biases**: an EXL3 vision linear's BF16 bias is added in float64 before the single bf16 rounding (HF's `nn.Linear` with bias does one rounding).
- **Positions** are absolute and identical in all three passes; the rings and conv states are per visit.

## Engine emulation

The REXL3 port makes lossy choices that differ from the rounding points above. The oracle can reproduce each one behind a flag; all flags are off by default and a fixture records its
setting as metadata `emulate` (`none`, `engine` for all, or a comma list). The Small's three flags carry over unchanged in definition; the Mini adds the fourth and the loop
rules. "The engine must round X" means: for the emulated oracle to match the engine up to its fixed fp32 summation order, the engine must store X at that width.

| Flag | Engine choice | Emulation (float64 otherwise) |
|---|---|---|
| `kv8` | The 8 global visits store K and V as int8 with a power-of-two scale per (token, KV head) (`cuda/csrc/nemotron_kv8.cuh`); every query, the writing step's own rows included, reads them back; the 40 sliding rings stay bf16. One page per global visit | After k̂ / v̂ (bf16): `e` = the smallest exponent with `amax / 2^e <= 127` from amax's fp32 bits, clamped to [-24, 8]; `q = rint(x / 2^e)` (nearest even) saturated to +-127; the value read is `q * 2^e`. Applied per global visit; may be switched off per visit (UNKNOWN-20) |
| `attn_bf16_p` | The attention kernel rounds the probabilities to bf16 for `P V` | Keys in absolute chunks (512 global, 256 sliding) of absolute 64-key tiles; per chunk an online softmax over its tiles in ascending order in the exp2 domain: `m' = max(m, tile max)`, `p = 2^(t - m')`, `l = l 2^(m - m') + sum p` (unrounded), `o = o 2^(m - m') + bf16(p) V`; chunk states folded left in ascending order; `out = bf16( o / l * sigmoid(gate) )` with the sink in `l`. The chunk plan is the kernel's to re-derive for GQA 3 (PORT-SPEC section 8) |
| `moe_fp16` | The EXL3 expert kernels store each GEMM input, the rotated `(x (*) suh) H / sqrt(128)` and the rotated `(act (*) suh_d) H / sqrt(128)`, as fp16; `g`, `u`, the activation and the expert output are rounded as above. Expert tensors are K = 4 or 5, `mul1` | The rotated input is rounded to fp32, then to fp16 (nearest even), before the `W_q` product |
| `dense_fp16` (was `exl3_fp16`) | The dense EXL3 linears (attention q, r, gate, k, v, o; unembed; vision; drafter) do the same: `xh = fp16( (x (*) suh) H / sqrt(128) )` computed in fp32, fp16 `mma` against the decoded fp16 weights, an fp32 sum of the K-split partials in order, then `(c H / sqrt(128)) * svh (+ bias)` in fp32 and one store in the consumer's dtype: bf16 for projections, fp32 for the unembed (`cuda/csrc/exl3_linear.cu:134-150, 186-224`) | Round `(x (*) suh) H / sqrt(128)` to fp32 then fp16 before the `W_q` product; the output transform stays float64 and is stored per consumer as in the table above **Implemented (`oracle/inkling2_ref.py` `Emulation.dense_fp16`, numerics version 2)** for every dense EXL3 linear the target oracle evaluates through `lin`: attention q, r, gate, k, v, `wo` and the unembed (the product with the decoded weight uses the fp16-rounded rotated input; output rounding as in the table above). Stay exact: BF16 tensors (router, `attn_sink_proj`, `weight_proj` taps, controllers, convs, queries, the embedding). Not covered: the vision tower and the drafter (`dspark_ref.py` has its own numerics and is unchanged; its linears are dense/EXL3-equivalent in its own tests). `--emulate engine` and the teacher-forcing gate's default variant (`kv8+attn_bf16_p+dense_fp16`) include it. |

Rules the engine must follow for the loop to match the emulated oracle:

1. The AttnRes stack holds `P` and the completed blocks as bf16; the MLP-mix input `partial` is fp32 and never stored in bf16.
2. A mix output is fp32 and reaches the next RMSNorm or controller unrounded; the message mix output is fp32; the blend is fp32 without FMA contraction; the RMSNorm after it stores bf16.
3. The controller's pair-norm output and `hdn` are bf16; `gain` and `forget` are fp32 from bf16 weights.
4. Conv states hold the raw bf16 inputs (exact); the K/V conv, `attn_sconv` and `mlp_sconv` multiply the fp32 tap sum by the fp32 constants of UNKNOWN-4.
5. Messages of pass `p` read the previous pass's `(blocks after the first, final P)`, both bf16 values; per-visit KV/ring/conv state is never shared between passes.

Everything else the engine does is fp32 with its own fixed summation order. The oracle does not emulate that order; `oracle/teacher_force.py` measures it as a noise floor instead (the oracle against a second fp32 implementation of itself).

Measured whole-forward effect (`docs/ORACLE-GAP.md`, record `2026-11-23-oracle-gap`, four prompts of 25 to 1,119 tokens, engine `7da8c61`): free-running through the 48 visits the engine is as far from the emulating oracle as the emulating oracle is from the exact one (hidden plane relative L2 0.26 at visit 36 of the long prompt against 0.27; 27 % of the router pairs flip against 28.5 %; logits relative L2 0.10 against 0.09), so the four emulated choices do not close the free-running gap (the fp32 summation order, amplified, is what is left); the engine's greedy tokens equal the oracle's on 64 of 64 positions; the engine's argmax differs from either oracle on 3 of 1,265 rows (prompt rows, oracle margin 0.10 to 0.22), the oracle's two numerics on 0.

## B lane (fp8 prefill): measured, not built

The B lane (plan row 7.3) was a proposed bit-changing, opt-in prefill (`REXL3_INKLING2_LOOP_PREFILL_LANE=fp8`, default `exact`) whose only change would have been the prefill GEMMs of the routed experts (gate, up, down) and of the dense EXL3 projections (q, r, gate, k, v, o); decode, the drafter, the vision tower, the unembed, the attention core and the glue would have kept the A lane's numerics above. It is **not built**: both conditions of its decision gate failed (`bench/records/2026-11-23-fp8-numerics`, `-fp8-potential`). What was measured, so that the choice is reproducible:

- **What would have been rounded where.** The rotated input `xh = (x . suh) H_K` to e4m3 (round to nearest even, saturating, `cvt.rn.satfinite.e4m3x2`) with one fp32 scale a row (applied in the epilogue; a scale per row and 128 columns is the variant `f8_blk128`), instead of fp16; the decoded codebook values `W_q` (fp16, exact) to e4m3 with no scale (a per-128 x 128-tile scale is `f8_row_tile`); fp32 accumulation, `H_N` and `svh` as before; the down projection's rotated activation the same.
- **Per op, on real inputs and real decoded weights** (`oracle/fp8_emu.py layers`, the 1,119-token prompt): relative L2 against the exact float64 product 1.6-4.3% (dense) and 2.8-3.6% (MoE gate / up / down) against the A lane's 0.009-0.024% and 0.015-0.020%: 60 to 181 times. The activations and the weights each contribute about 70% of the total; per-row, per-block and per-tile scales change nothing (the rotated inputs have no outliers; e4m3 has 3 mantissa bits, 2.65% rms on a Gaussian).
- **Whole forward** (`fp8_emu.py forward`, three short prompts, teacher forced): the hidden plane's deviation added to the A lane's is 1.5 to 2.6 times the A lane's own deviation from the exact oracle (13-14 times at visit 1, saturating as the model amplifies both), in 0 of 48 visits within it; KL(exact || run) 0.026 against 0.0028 (A lane) on the chat prompts and 0.0007 against 0.0002 on `raw-counting`; the argmax differs from the exact oracle on 7 of 139 rows (the A lane: 0).


## B lane: expert skipping (oracle option, measured, not built)

`Emulation.expert_skip` (threshold `t`, 0 = off) with `skip_renorm` is the oracle's model of the plan's adaptive expert skipping (`docs/C4-DECODE-PLAN.md` section 6). It is lossy, not part of `engine()` and not one of `NAMES`; spelled `expert_skip=0.2` and `skip_renorm` in `Emulation.parse` (`engine,expert_skip=0.2,skip_renorm` is the A lane with renormalised skipping at 0.2), and recorded in a fixture's `emulate` tag. Exactly what is dropped, where:

- **Where**: the MoE of every layer pass (48 a token), on every row it processes (prefill and decode alike: the oracle has one code path; `oracle/skip_study.py forward --decode-only` prefills with the A lane and skips only in the decode steps, which is the engine lever's domain).
- **What**: of a row's top-8 picks (router weights `w_i = fp32(8 s_i / (sum s + 1e-20))`, sum 8), those with `w_i < t * max_j w_j`; the dropped expert is not evaluated for that row. The top pick always stays (`0 < t <= 1`). Decided per row from the row's own weights: a row's output does not depend on the window or batch it is in. A pick dropped for one row is dropped even where its expert is read for another row (the byte saving is by the union rule; the numerics are per row).
- **Weights**: `plain` keeps the surviving weights as they are (the dropped contribution is simply missing); `renorm` multiplies them by `sum(w) / sum(kept w)`, fp32-rounded, so the row sums to 8 again. Measured: renormalising is worse (`2026-11-23-skip-study`).
- **Untouched**: the router (scores, selection, bias, margins) and the recorded `router_idx` / `router_w` / `router_margin` planes are the router's own, so a run's routes are comparable with the A lane's and the dropped picks can be recounted from them (`skip_picks`); the expert GEMMs of the kept picks, the float64 sum, the single bf16 rounding and the `mlp_sconv` after it are the A lane's. Off (`t = 0`) is bit-identical to the oracle without the option (`oracle/tests/test_expert_skip.py`); a threshold that drops nothing, run through the skip path, equals the engine fixture bit for bit (`skip_study.py control`).
- **Measured** (`bench/records/2026-11-23-skip-study/`, three short prompts, teacher forced on the A lane's tokens): on every row, t = 0.2 renorm: KL(A || run) mean 0.148 over 139 rows, top-1 122/139; with the prompt prefilled exactly and only the decode rows skipping: KL mean 0.0022 over 53 rows, 2 argmax flips (A margins 0.03, 0.39); t = 0.3 decode-only 0.0135 over 38 rows. Gate and weaknesses are in the record's README.


## Drafter rounding points

`oracle/dspark_ref.py` (PORT-SPEC section 4, VLM `2b55f82b`). Everything else is float64 from the stored values; the EXL3 weights are the exactly decoded ones.

- `alpha = fp32(softmax(fuse_weight))` over the 4 taps (rows: draft layers 0-2, Markov head); feature mix + `dflare_norm`: bf16 (sum in float64).
- Context K/V: `k_raw` and `v` bf16; `k_norm_ctx` output bf16; RoPE output bf16.
- RoPE table: `inv_freq = fp32( 1 / fp32(theta^(2i/D)) )` with theta = 1e6, angle `fp32(pos * inv_freq)`, `fp32(cos)`, `fp32(sin)`.
- Block: `embed_norm(embed[anchor])` and the layer-0 `input_layernorm` bf16; q, k, v bf16; q/k norm bf16; RoPE bf16; attention output bf16 (exact float64 softmax, non-causal over the context window plus the block).
- `o_proj` and `down_proj` outputs bf16 (TP1: one rounding, no rank partials); residual stored as `bf16( residual + branch )`, and the following norm (`post_attention_layernorm`, the next `input_layernorm`, `norm`) reads that bf16 residual (UNKNOWN-21).
- MLP: `g`, `u` bf16; `silu(g) * u` bf16.
- Markov head: `fold_W` and `fold_b` bf16 from float64 products of the decoded weights (UNKNOWN-17, variant a); `ctx_i` fp32 (fp32 product, then + bias in fp32); base logits bf16; per step `raw` fp32 (fp32 product, then + `ctx_i`); `bf16(raw)` -> sigmoid / tanh each bf16; `g * state`, `1 - g`, `(1 - g) * c` and the new state bf16; `latent = bf16(tanh(o))`; bias bf16; `bf16(base + bias)`, then the soft-capped logit bf16.
- Draft: first maximum over `[0, 200058)`, NaN as -inf (UNKNOWN-14).
